## What actually moved

**1. Amazon finished the OpenAI cheque early — and quietly.**
The single biggest confirmed outlay of the window. Amazon's 10-Q (filed 2026-07-31) discloses
that *subsequent to June 30* it funded the remaining **$21.3bn** of its $35bn OpenAI equity
commitment, on top of **$13.7bn** in Q2 — completing the full **$50bn** for ~5% of OpenAI.
The tell is *why*: the back-end $35bn was contractually contingent on an OpenAI IPO **or**
specified milestones, and Amazon paid anyway with neither publicly confirmed. Either a
milestone was hit privately or Amazon wanted the shares before an IPO reprices them. The same
filing discloses a separate, previously unquantified **$10.0bn into Anthropic** nonvoting
preferred in Q2. Amazon's private-company equity carrying value is now **$122.3bn** — this is
the largest corporate balance-sheet position in frontier AI anywhere, and it is being built in
tranches disclosed only in 10-Q footnotes, not press releases. Read the footnotes, not the wire.

**2. NVIDIA is buying the customer base, not just selling to it — and did it four times in 22 days.**
NAVER ($1bn for ~4.5%, becoming the #3 shareholder, funding a 55MW→200MW AI factory alongside
Brookfield's up-to-$9bn), Safe Superintelligence (size undisclosed by NVIDIA; ~$5bn per
Bloomberg), Nebius (first public 13G — 9.3%, though the cash actually went out in March), and
Volta Infra (a seven-month-old GPU-financing startup at a $2.4bn valuation). Three distinct
patterns in one month: **sovereign-AI equity** (NAVER), **frontier-lab optionality** (SSI), and
**vendor-financed demand** (Nebius, Volta). The Volta deal is the most instructive — NVIDIA is
now seeding the *financing layer* that lets smaller buyers afford its chips, with a bank pool
(Azora, ~$5bn) and a ~$10bn end-customer contract stacked on a $300m equity round.

**3. Meta committed ~$61bn+ to physical datacenter capacity in three weeks, in three different ownership structures.**
Alberta (CAD $13bn / ~$9.2bn, 1GW, **100% Meta-owned**), Richland Parish "Hyperion" (Louisiana
confirms **>$50bn**, 5GW — a ~5x expansion of the Dec-2024 commitment), and El Paso (Meta takes
only **20%**, BlackRock funds 80%, Meta contributes ~$2.3bn of assets and takes ~$1bn back at
close while carrying up to ~$13bn of residual-value guarantees). The El Paso structure — the
second after the Oct-2025 Louisiana venture — is the signal: Meta is deliberately moving the
heaviest capital off its own balance sheet into asset-manager JVs while keeping the compute.
Watch whether the *next* campus is 20%-owned too.
